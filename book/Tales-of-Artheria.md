### Amethyst Peak[^nosaj]

**Amethyst Peak’s Goal**  
To prosper in destitution is the mark of a strong soul. One must bear an unworldly strength and indomitable will to truly live.

**History**  
Known as the City of Dragons, Amethyst Peak is a kingdom that sits atop Artheria's tallest volcano. It was founded by Anarch, the Deity of Lachesism, thousands of years ago. The location was home to an abundance of dragons, which Anarch associated with absolute power after witnessing the magic possessed by the Elder Dragons of Corvathe. He aimed to tame these dragons in an attempt to harness their magic.

> ⚖ **Sources disagree.** The *Church of Anarch* says AP was founded “thousands of years ago, in the Ice”. *Talar the Human* dates it to **Year 1000**. The *Ranlean sages* date the “true city” to when Ranlean and Aralon first met it, after the thaw. The *Church* also calls this the tallest volcano in the world. *Sailors* who have seen Silverpeak disagree.

**The Populace**  
It is home to many Emberkin and Dragon Descendants.  
Mostly humans, a few elves. Drakari.

**The Kingdom: From a one man village to a thriving Kingdom State.**  
A millennia later, though Anarch has since dropped his draconic venture, Amethyst Peak still reigns as a symbol of strength and power. Many have tried to overtake the kingdom and seize control of the dragons, but have all fallen to fearsome warriors.

**Previous Kings**

**Obsidian**  
Amethyst Peaks’s economy is largely based on its export of Obsidian armors and swords. The most luxurious armaments being Dragonbreathe Crafted weapons. Another key export is Sulfur, which is also produced into Batteries for Ranlean. It can be made into Sulfuric Acid, Oil Refinement and Water Processing Products. For Aralon’s agriculture, sulfur can also be made into Fertilizer and Fungicide. In house it’s used for Mineral Extraction and to create poison gas. It was also used as a kind of fire weapon that could continue to burn in water, known as the Flames of Amnesty.

Aside from that, the Peak also exports meat to Aralon and Ranlean, specifically Goat, Lamb and Rabbit meat. Hunters gather Bear, Bobcat, Rodent and various cave dwelling animal meat at a higher price. Though the premium product is Dragon Meat. This meat is rare, as it’s collected from aging dragons that have passed away from natural means. A dragon is never killed for this purpose, as dictated by the Church of Anarch.

Dragon Stages of Life  
Egg \- 1 year incubation period. They are kept warm in an incubator with high temperatures.

Wyrmling \-  When a dragon leaves their egg, they are known as wyrmlings. Their scales begin to take a color. They require a lot of care and attention, needing an older dragon as a parental figure. Introduction to sentients is key in this stage of life. A wyrmling not socialized at this point in time will grow to see sentients as a food source, and not a symbiotic partner.

Wyrmlings can occasionally breathe fire, but it’s uncontrolled. Hence, why scale nurseries exist. Often they hurt themselves as they don’t have the technique learned.

Young \- A youngling reaches this age when they can at will, breathe fire and can fly for short bursts of time. This is usually when they’re trained for war.

Adult \- These dragons are fully grown, and at the height of their mobility. They serve on the dragon force until stated otherwise.  
Elder \- You know they’re in their elder stages when you can see the graying edges of their scales, and their slower movements.

Oddly enough, the strength of their breath is strongest in this stage of life.

Elder dragons are retired, and enjoy a life of peace with their families. There are some elder dragons still serving in the military.

Passing/Funeral  
Dragons close to passing have the inevitable weakening of their breathe. Fire breathers get cooler and cooler.

Dragons often adopt characteristics of their element.  
The majority of fire breathing dragons keep a temperature of about 70C (154F). As such, Dragons are hot to the touch and their nests are often made for comfort, not to keep warm. Their lairs are usually somewhat charred. They enjoy rinsing in lava for brief periods of time. They do not bathe in the lava. Although these beasts are resistant to high temperatures, they are not immune to its melting point. High temperatures will soften the scales and reptilian hide.

Their ‘baths’ consist more of ‘fire grooming’, where the parent dragon breathes fire on their offspring periodically, until their bodies are free of debris.

Dragon fire breath is usually red/orange, some dragon fire burns blue. This is due to higher oxygen present in their bile organs. It is not known if this is a hereditary trait or one that comes from health habits.

Anarch’s dragon was known to have blue flame. But that is up to debate, as the documentation is poor. Any and all documents of Anarch are closely protected by the Church of Anarch, in their library catacombs.

In the case of the water breathing and acid breathing dragons:  
Water breathing dragons are moist to the touch. They run a little hotter than humans, (105F). Their nests are usually damp.

The recorded water dragons that lived in Amethyst Peak required a lot more water than their other counterparts. As such, the owners had to live on ground level, creating artificial ponds for the dragons to bathe and rest in.da

Acid Breathers are rare, but are heavily documented from this one example. Their lairs are usually caustic and hard to breathe in. nesting was hard to come by, often being melted within a season. ### Elves

Elves come from a primitive homeworld. Due to their long lives of 1000 years, they have witnessed many events. One event being the creation of humans. Their literature is considered to be of high quality to scholars and archeologists as their cultural pride made their content highly researched and valued to be as accurate as possible.

After the creation of humans, the elves have become mostly seclusive to their own. This was due to fear of the unknown, as well as humans having an appearance weirdly uncanny to elves, except for the ears and height.

Elves live in secluded villages and towns that are difficult to reach due to predators in their past. These settlements are often protected by means of magic as a secondary line of defense.

Of all the races, elves are considered the most unforgiving, holding grudges for centuries.They attack beastmen on sight and see humans as ‘inbred elves’. They are hostile towards orcs due to their learned history of raiding and war. While they find Infernlings unsightly, they do enjoy long conversations with them about magic.

They enjoy halflings, taking kinship in their long lifespan of about 400 years. Most agree that they are undisciplined, compared to the common elf.

The Elven Capital is where the elves believe the Travelers first landed on Artheria. The heart of the city is an academy. In this academy, there is a structure made of metal and rust, believed to house all of the knowledge of the Fairies and the Elven Ancestors. This structure is guarded by a small army of metal golems, guided by the wielder of a circlet. The circlet is worn by the Elven King.

The structure is off limits to the local populace, guarded by the Royal Army. Any attempt to break past the gates is met with death.

There have been attempts to study the structure; all met with destruction by the hands of the golem guards. It seems only the wielder of a circlet can bypass the guards. In Elven history, a queen was brave enough to enter the structure with a 2nd circlet, the queen’s circlet, never to return. Some elven archeologists say that most likely part of the structure collapsed, killing the brave queen. With no one else able to enter except for the king, she was doomed. It would have been foolish for the leader of the elven society to go after her, leaving the throne open. There was also the chance of the last circlet disappearing with the king, leaving the structure’s only key into the structure gone forever.

In elven history, the travelers were mostly elves and fairies working together. Archeologists say that a fairy may be the key to entering the structure without the need of a circlet. But with the extinction of fairies, that method too, is no longer possible.

Elves look down on other elves that regularly interact with other races, as they see that their society is everything an elf could ask for. “City elves must enjoy living in the slums that only a mixed society can afford.”

To keep updated in continent news, Elves will travel to other elf-populated towns to converse and keep each other safe. This creates an odd gray area for elves that live in the city, because how else would their ‘more pure’ cohorts learn of continent affairs if not for them? Elves commonly have identity issues that deal with tradition versus modernity.

Depending on where they live, elves have been noted to absorb the elements around them, their magic casting adopting the texture and color of where they’ve been raised. The Elves that spent their childhood in the desert have a sandy feel to them; their forest relatives, casting with the appearance of leaves and twigs.

Elves are practically humans, but their relationship with mana makes them live longer. Their bodies are more elongated, their longevity up to thousands of years. They physically do not age, but instead their mana ages over time. Other mana users can sense this. Mana users remark that older elves, if not taken care of, feel greasy, and smell like older humans.

> ⚖ **Sources disagree.** The *Elven archivists* record a lifespan of 1000 years. *Halfling legends*, awed, say “thousands”.

Elves usually live 1000 years. By their 900th year, their magic begins to fade. By 950, they can no longer cast magic. They begin to lose perception of time. They begin to fade, appearing like a ghost with a glowing center. They meditate for one last time. When this happens, they fade away, becoming part of their biome. Wood elves turn into wood, Tundra elves become ice. After death, a ritual is held. In this ritual, friends and family of the deceased are taken to the wildlands closest to where they died. The remains of the deceased are mixed back into the world. A bonfire is started, and the personal belongings of the deceased are burned. Whatever is left in the bonfire’s ashes is melted down or given to the next of kin. The melting down is seen as another act of becoming one with the land once again.

### Beastmen

After the war along the giants in ancient history, Beastmen were hunted due to their former alliance with the giants. They took shelter in the wilds where no other race could survive. They fought with one another for leadership, but that endeavor has since dissolved as they adapted to their new surroundings.

Now the beastmen have found purpose in taming animals and becoming close to animals that closely resemble them as well as other beasts. It is common for those from the feather clan to have a roost of eagles and falcons, and so on.

Every 4 years, beastmen from around the continent will travel to a competition sponsored by a beastman settlement to see who can win the beastman artifact for their tribe. The Artifact is a kind of cloak that signifies the clan that is most ready for war. The competition became a regular event to control the beastmen’s hunger for war, without reducing their already dwindling population.

The occasional beastman can be found in mixed society, but only if they are traveling with their ‘herd’ or ‘flock’. They are more trusted than orcs, but not by much. Still, if in need for a finely tamed creature, a beastman’s bond has no competition in quality.

Beastmen come in all shapes and sizes, living in tribes where the majority is one specific subculture of beast. The beast they have similarities to is the creature they worship. Their method of worship varies greatly between tribes. Some capture and release their totem as a rite of passage, some eat their totem. Often their totem is nearby or tamed into servitude, depending on the animal’s nature and chance of predating the tribe.

Beastmen who try to procreate with another beast culture are met with disappointment. Their genes are not fit. Often these offspring are cast out. Some speculate that this practice is why there are so many odd creatures in the wilds.

### Halflings

For a time, halflings were resentful of their captors, the beastmen and the elves. But that dissent disappeared with time. As they live long lives compared to most other races, they have used that time to reflect and forgive. Most halflings live peaceful lives, drinking and eating for most of their days, stirring up good times with instruments in hand. This race works hard just as much as they play hard, spending 2 days of the week working tirelessly in the mines, inventing, and cooking up delicious food. Being natives of Artheria, they have the most knowledge of cuisine. All throughout the land, even in elvish society, they have restaurants that sell their food. Legend has it that a small number of large conflicts have been solved by a bowl of halfling stew.

As such, most other races leave halflings alone, actively seeking them out for recipes and cooking advice. But make no mistake, a halfling can be useful in situations that require a small body. Halflings have been known to join the Adventurer’s Guild for a more exciting lifestyle, specifically in asset recollection or exploration. A few halflings know their way around the blade.

### Infernlings

Born from the First Warlock, Infernlings are looked down upon in human society and attacked on sight by Orcs. Their name comes from a term meaning ‘low’ or ‘deep’, as if the power given to them comes from an obscure entity that clawled from the dark depths of a terrible place. They have inherited their magical ability from the elves as was guaranteed in the promise, at the cost of their appearance. They are regarded as ill omens, their dark magic a reason for vigilance and caution. Most uneducated people in smaller towns are afraid of them on appearance alone, much like orcs or beastmen.  
It is unclear which powerful entity their common ancestor gave his soul to, but a side effect of their gift is that they are strongly resistant to fire. An Infernling can withstand boiling temperatures for hours and can even stand on fire for a long period of time before being burned.

If an Infernling is in a society that permits their residence, they are more often than not placed in the more dangerous part of town. Still, their resistance to heat makes them prized as firefighters. Their given talent for magic makes them excellent magic users, on par with elves. Unlike elves, the appearance of their casting is alarming. Their bright yellow or red eyes flicker with the shimmer of fire, their spellcasting voice deepens to an inhuman low pitch. Some say that the most powerful spellcasters dim the area of light, making their spells that much more intimidating.

During their inception as a people, some Infernlings report visions of crossing into the dark planes, soaking in the mana of dark spirits. Their skin can range from a bright red to purple due to the alien habitat they were forced to live in, A kind of place where birds, fish and reptiles thrived. The brightness of their skin displayed that they are masters of fleeing. Their horns, from the animals that butt heads. Their small control of fire and vestigial tails, a remnant of the cold dark place their former spirits chose to live in.

### Orcs

Orcs came into being from Humans. Humans used the knowledge of the fairies, using what they called ‘Ashen Magic’ to try and regain the ability to use magic. This was combined with the technology invented in Artheria. The spell ultimately failed, creating orcs. The spell corrupted the humans who ran the experiment, mutating them into a new type of sentient being.

> ⚖ **Sources disagree.** *Quimzy the Orc* insists orcs carry elven blood too (“we are older than your shame”). *Talar the Human* says orcs came from humans alone.

Because of their degenerative bloodline, the majority of orcs are unintelligent and aggressive, making them the most despised of all the races throughout the land. They are unwelcome in most societies, the intelligent ones having to cover the color of their greenish skin.

The only accepted orcs are those of nobility, well known throughout the land as charming, strong, intelligent individuals capable of anything they set their endeavors on, provided they show proof of legitimacy. Often these nobility will go out of their way to dress in the best quality garments or armor they can find, decorated with their house’s symbol.  
Despite this, Infernlings still look down upon them as much as other societies look down on them due to the current war.

Fighting an orc in combat is just as dangerous as fighting a wild animal, their natural physique letting them fight longer than even beastmen.

Orcs are often vying to show who the most capable house members are, joining any competition that will bring honor to their name. Some orcs attribute this to their nature, saying that even the most unintelligent orc wants to prove their might in combat for their tribe.

Orcish Colors  
Skin can range from Human colors to dark green, almost black olive skin tone.  
Degenerate Orcs choose to wear what is available. Their ripped clothing and discarded armors are stolen and poorly sewn together.

Noble Orcs choose to adorn their bodies and jewelries with colors that match their skin tone. Major colors are red, orange, brown, gray and gold.

Because of their population being majority degenerative orcs who are more brawn than brain, the Noble orcs (despite their Intelligence and mental adaptability on par with Elves), must prove their strength to their warring kin. Their armor is fierce, often blood soaked, battle worn and depicts the brutalism of war. The most savage of Orc tribes will have flags of living enemies, with limbs broken, their skin flayed, etc. kept alive until a suitable replacement can be broken down and dismembered in front of enemies.

The Noble Orcs in discussion with other intellectuals, despise this method of control. But better they do this, than someone who does this in joy. It is a necessary evil in the most brutal of Orc clans.

The more civil orc clans merely wish to display their strength amongst each other. Some Nobility were successful in channeling their habits to constructive events such as sports rallies. Thus, they can be crowned the strongest in a division, their competitiveness slaked.

### The Ephemerals of Nepenthe

---

**01 The Island of Nepenthe**  
Nepenthe was a self-sufficient village on a secluded island. It was once filled with a youthful whimsy and its inhabitants often lived long, healthy lives. The land was fruitful and the plants were tinted with brilliance.  
However, Nepenthe and all of its radiance faded away after it was invaded by the Arresters. The Arresters enslaved the aboriginals and this would be their life until the conquistadors could find the Seed of Thithonus.

**02 The Seed of Thithonus**  
Thithonus was once a mortal man who was granted eternal. life. Unfortunately, he was not blessed with eternal youth and he is now forced to live an endless life in old age. He has since aged to a point where he can no longer move his body. Legends state that, in an attempt to combat his curse, he created a seed that would grant him eternal youth. Although it is unconfirmed if he was successful in this endeavor, the seed is thought to reside in Nepenthe.

**03 Invasion of the Arresters**  
The Arresters are a group of conquistadors on a quest to find the Seed of Thithonus. They believed that, with the power of immortality, they'd be able to grow their armies and conquer all of man.  
The search for the seed had led them to Nepenthe where they pillaged the land and enslaved its inhabitants. They are determined to find the seed and until then, they aim to wreak havoc on the island with hopes that the seed will reveal itself.

**04 Anarch, Vellichor, & Petrichor**  
Among the tribe were three young Lumari who differed from the others: Anarch, Vellichor, and his sister Petrichor. They were born into a life of slavery, but were convinced that this was not the life they were meant to live. Anarch was rebellious in nature and often defied the Arresters and their demands. This behavior would have been his undoing if it wasn't for his best friend, Vellichor, a calm and collected scholar. Petrichor, unlike the other two, was an extrovert who was enamored by her very existence and lived a life of unconditional compassion and kindness.

> ⚖ **Sources disagree.** The *Church of Anarch* calls Anarch a born deity. *Nepenthe’s own tradition* remembers a Lumari slave who drank immortal sap. *Anarch’s Journals* never mention Nepenthe.

**05 The Evergreen Sapling**  
Petrichor, with her charming naivety, frequently described the beauty of a tree that would grow if the Seed Thithonus had ever been planted. Vellichor, however, found truth in this dream despite his hyper- logical mindset.  
Believing that this was their only way of surviving an escape, Anarch and Vellichor searched all of Nepenthe until they found what must be the immortal tree that Petrichor envisioned. It was colossal in size and was decorated with leaves so vivid they seemed to give off a glow. Its trunk was that of a sapling and the only evidence of age was its size.

**06 Leap of Faith**  
During their search for the mythical tree, the trio was unknowingly followed by an Arrester who was unsure of their intent. Anarch grew suspicious after spotting an extra set of footsteps on their trail. Upon the potential discovery of the Evergreen Sapling, Anarch heard the approaching Arrester and felt that they needed to act quickly. He took a chance, stabbed the trunk, and licked the sap off of his blade. He urged the other two to do the same and jumped off the 100 foot cliff. into the ocean. Vellichor had attempted to collect as much sap into a vial when the Arrester had called out to them. He and Petrichor both sipped from the vial before he grabbed her and jumped.

**The Ephemerals**  
After witnessing the trio make their jump, the Arrester chased after them towards the edge of the cliff. He peered over the edge, examined the drop, and saw no signs of life in the ocean nor did he see any chance of survival. He reported back to his leader who labeled them "The Ephemerals" due to their short-lived existence.  
Back at the cliff side, bubbles rose to the surface of the water. Through the ripples in the sea, the trio emerged, seemingly unscathed. They swam to the nearest shore where they would finally experience life. This marked the immortal rebirth of the Ephemerals of Nepenthe.

**The Ballad of Kazka** *(a bard’s retelling; details differ from Cerwind’s account above)*  
The night was thick with darkness as Kazka sat slumped at the bar, drowning his sorrows in a sea of ale. Born in Aralon, the son of a former adventurer, he had inherited a legacy of daring and danger. But it was a pact with a mysterious being, introduced to him by his father, that had brought misfortune and misery to Kazka's life. The pain inflicted upon him and those around him was crippling, a constant reminder of the pact's malevolent power.

Desperate for salvation, Kazka turned to Petrichor, the deity of Aralon, and fervently prayed for guidance. In his unwavering devotion, he became a Paladin, dedicating himself to serving Petrichor's righteous cause. Seeking a way out of Aralon, he decided to join the adventurer's guild, hoping to find purpose and redemption beyond the borders of his troubled homeland.

But as he approached the guild, he caught a glimpse into the minds of the first group he encountered. They saw Kazka as nothing more than cannon fodder, a disposable human shield to be thrown into the fray. Disheartened but undeterred, he sought out another group, one comprised of inexperienced adventurers with menial tasks ahead of them—clearing out rats in the sewers, collecting flowers, and the like.

Joining the Adventurer's Guild as a young man, Kazka met Gruum, Jerry, and Fumir, forming a bond that would reshape their lives. Together, they embarked on countless quests and perilous adventures, their deeds spreading far and wide. The four became renowned throughout the land, their exploits earning them the moniker "The Band."

But the tale of The Band was not one of unending triumph. Tragedy befell them one by one, shattering their unity and testing Kazka's spirit. Jerry met his demise in a cataclysmic explosion, collapsing a cave filled with monsters. Gruum, facing bitter cold and encased in fallen ice, succumbed to death's icy grip. Fumir, the last remaining companion, left Kazka without a word, disappearing into the night, leaving behind a void of unanswered questions.

Lost and broken, Kazka turned to the solace of spirits, drowning his sorrows in the darkest corners of the taverns that dotted the towns he passed through. For years, he wandered aimlessly, a ghost of his former self, his pain etched deeply into every line of his weathered face. By the time the Einkhala Mission presented itself, he was 38 years old, wearied by the weight of his own existence.

It was in the desert village of Sornes, as the moon cast a pale glow over the sandy landscape, that Kazka found himself in a dimly lit bar, drowning his memories. That night, fate would intervene in the form of Cerwind, a young Owlkin Ranger, and a small human child named Terry. Roused from his stupor, Kazka was compelled to listen as they explained their desperate need for him to retrieve the Einkhala—an artifact last mentioned during the heyday of The Band. They were members of Pallasite, an enigmatic group of researchers, and the tusks of the Einkhala held crucial significance for their magical studies.

Reluctantly, Kazka accepted their proposition, his weary heart yearning for any glimmer of hope. In return for the Einkhala's tusks, Pallasite promised to provide him with information about Fumir, the one person he longed to meet again. And so, under the cloak of night, they embarked on a treacherous seven-day journey northeast, towards Straphan Forest.

With the moon casting a dim glow over the landscape, Kazka and his newfound companions, Cerwind and the young scholar Terry, stood at a crucial crossroads. Before them lay two paths: the long, safe route, and the shorter, treacherous cliffside ascent. The urgency of their mission propelled them to make a daring choice—the cliff was their path to take.

Heart pounding, muscles straining, Kazka led the way, scaling the near-vertical cliff with sheer determination. Every foothold and handhold counted, as they defied gravity and embraced the thrill of risk. As they ascended, the winds whispered secrets of ancient perils and unseen dangers that awaited them on their journey.

Days turned to nights and back again as they ventured forth, their footsteps guided by the slivers of shade offered by the scorching sun. But danger was never far behind. Suddenly, their path was beset by two colossal creatures—giant lizards lunging at them with jaws agape. The group fought valiantly, their blades clashing with reptilian scales. However, their victory was swiftly replaced by perplexity as they realized the lizards were nothing more than cunningly crafted mechanical constructs, mere facades concealing a more sinister force at play.

The revelation of their adversaries being robotic sent shivers down their spines. The true danger lurking in their path became palpable, instilling a sense of urgency within their ranks. Two days later, as they rested and conversed, Cerwind shared his dreams of becoming a renowned adventurer, walking in the footsteps of the legendary Kazka himself. Terry, driven by his scholarly pursuits, spoke passionately of his research for Pallasite, delving into the mysteries of the magical arts.

Their peaceful conversation was abruptly shattered as the robotic lizards reemerged, attacking with a vengeance. In the chaos that ensued, Terry found himself trapped in his tent, unable to escape the relentless assault. Helplessly, Kazka and Cerwind fought to protect their fallen comrade, but it was too late. Terry succumbed to the venomous bites of the mechanical monstrosities.

Grief-stricken yet resolute, Cerwind vowed to honor Terry's memory. He wrapped the fallen scholar's body in a makeshift shroud of tent cloth, carrying the weight of a promise made to deliver Terry back to his people. With each step, their resolve to complete their mission burned brighter, fueled by a mixture of vengeance and the determination to see their task through to the end.

Two days passed, and the journey led them to the fabled Ancient Ruins—an enigmatic testament to forgotten civilizations and untold secrets. They navigated treacherous fire traps and cunning arrow mechanisms, their every move dictated by the wisdom of survival. Climbing higher, they reached the apex of an ancient tower, where a legendary gem awaited them. Three blades, each adorned with a gem of radiant power, shimmered in the dim light. Instinct guided Kazka's hand as he reached for the green gem, a choice that seemed to resonate with his very soul.

Having acquired the gem, they wasted no time lingering in the ruins. Their quest was far from over, and the Straphan Forest beckoned them, promising both answers and challenges on their path. With determination etched upon their faces, they embarked once more into the unknown, hearts resolute as they continued their arduous journey.

Four days later, their travels amounted to fifteen days of unyielding determination and swift footsteps. The path they had chosen, fraught with peril and loss, had brought them this far.

Emerging from the depths of their arduous journey, Kazka, Cerwind, and their burdened souls arrived at the majestic Straphan Forest. As they stepped into the verdant expanse, their senses heightened, and they were greeted by a captivating sight. In the distance, a pack of red wolves, their coats blazing like the setting sun, moved with fluid grace, led by a magnificent white wolf—a beacon of untamed power.

Aware of the time-sensitive nature of their mission, they set up camp and patiently awaited the arrival of the Einkhala. The passing days weighed heavily upon them, amplified by the stench of decay emanating from Terry's rotting body, a grim reminder of their fallen comrade's fate.

During their wait, Cerwind unveiled his trump card—a quiver of Tranquilizer Arrows. Crafted with precision, only three of these arrows needed to find their mark to bring down the elusive Einkhala. Then, in a sudden burst of anticipation, the Einkhala revealed itself, its presence like a whisper carried by the wind. Swift and elusive, it defied their expectations, challenging their resolve. In that crucial moment, Kazka tapped into the wellspring of his divine connection, summoning a mystical steed—a horse imbued with black tentacles that swirled around its powerful form.

Mounted upon his otherworldly steed, Kazka and Cerwind pursued the Einkhala with an unwavering determination. The chase unfolded amidst a chaotic ballet of arrows and fangs, as Cerwind unleashed his tranquilizer-laden arsenal upon both the swift Einkhala and the relentless wolves that trailed its wake.

At breakneck speed, Kazka guided the horse, its hooves thundering against the forest floor, deftly maneuvering through the dense foliage. They weaved between towering trees, narrowly avoiding catastrophic collisions, as their moving battle raged on. Arrows whistled through the air, piercing the flesh of both prey and predator.

And then, as destiny aligned with their desperate endeavor, the third tranquilizer arrow struck true. The Einkhala, its strength waning, slowed its frenzied sprint and careened into the riverbank, its once unyielding spirit now subdued. It was a moment of triumph amidst chaos.

With the green blade gripped firmly in his hand, Kazka approached the subdued Einkhala. The air crackled with anticipation as the blade, infused with the power of Petrichor, sizzled through the air, severing the tusks from the fallen beast. A surge of accomplishment washed over them—a tangible symbol of their perseverance and sacrifice.

As the adrenaline of their recent victory subsided, Kazka found himself faced with an unexpected encounter. A baby Einkhala, small and vulnerable, emerged from the shadows, attempting to defend its fallen mother. Its clumsy attempts at aggression melted away any lingering hostility within Kazka's heart. He realized that this young creature mourned the loss of its perceived protector.

Moved by compassion, Kazka gently disarmed the baby Einkhala, understanding its misguided intentions. With a heavy heart, he watched as the baby Einkhala wailed in sorrow, its world forever changed. Determined to honor its fallen mother's memory, Kazka and Cerwind gathered their belongings, leaving the forest behind as they embarked on a new chapter of their journey.

Their path led them to the vibrant city of Ranlean, where Cerwind and Terry had originated. She extended a small bag brimming with coins and jewels, a gesture of gratitude for their success in obtaining the Einkhala tusks. With newfound purpose in his stride, Kazka bid Cerwind farewell. Their hands clasped in a gesture of understanding and support, Cerwind headed to address the sorrowful chapter that Terry's death had brought upon them. Meanwhile, Kazka set his sights on the path ahead, fueled by the knowledge that the opportunity to reunite with Fumir awaited him.

Armed with a map, a bag of provisions, and a mixture of determination and trepidation, Kazka ventured forth, his previous and future business intertwining within his thoughts. The knowledge that he could once again cross paths with Fumir, the enigmatic figure from his past, fueled his resolve.

The road stretched out before Kazka, winding and uncertain. Yet, armed with the memories of his fallen companions and the promise of a reunion, he pressed forward, ready to face the trials and revelations that lay ahead.

**The Patron**  
From an unfathomable realm of agony, suffering, and perpetual devastation, the Patron was summoned into existence. This ethereal entity, akin to a spirit, possessed the ability to alter its form at will. Its essence exuded a trinity of mana, radiating hues of green, purple, and red. Manifesting as a dark mass, intertwined with an array of fiery colors, it bore horns and tails.  
Upon entering the realm of Artheria, the Patron found respite from ceaseless torment. This new world appeared akin to a paradisiacal haven. Endowed with telekinetic powers, the Patron possessed the uncanny ability to discern the intentions of other living beings. Instead of relying on verbal discourse, it communicated through emotions, as such was the custom within its original plane. Communication there thrived solely through the language of annihilation, destroy or be destroyed.  
The summoning of this entity into the mortal realm was the work of **The Scholar**, a human wizard who delved into forbidden fairy lore. The Patron was brought forth with the purpose of safeguarding humanity from the merciless onslaught of the Orcs, who sought to inflict genocide upon their kind. The Patron's native realm was perpetually engulfed in an invisible inferno, which the Scholar harnessed to provide warmth to the humans during the Era of Ice.  
Initially, the Scholar restrained the Patron with ethereal chains, endeavoring to establish communication with the colossal spirit. Once a dialogue could be established, the Scholar forged a pact, communicating through the medium of emotions.  
The agreement was struck. In exchange for the Patron's formidable abilities, the Scholar committed to maintaining the presence of the being in the mortal realm, safeguarding the gates through which it had entered for as long as possible.  
At least, that was the Scholar's understanding.  
The Patron harbored reservations. and mistrust towards the diminutive creature and its immense power, deceived the Scholar.  
The pact was sealed, yet the price to sustain the gates cursed the bloodline of the Scholar for generations to come. The once clear skies became shrouded in a somber gray hue, not from smoke but from an unending swarm of lesser demons hailing from the Patron's abode.  
On that fateful day, the Orcish war came to an abrupt halt. Their numbers decimated by the relentless onslaught of the demons that descended upon them. When the orcs finally relented, the demons sought to remain in this newfound paradise.  
The exact method employed by the Scholar to banish the demons remains shrouded in mystery. Nonetheless, the Scholar managed to accomplish this feat, albeit leaving two portals in their wake: the Noctavel portal and the Emberkin Portal. Nevertheless, the damage had been done. Inexplicable events began to unfold across the world, with reports of nightmarish creatures spreading chaos, death, and madness.  
The fate of the Patron after the pact's inception remains unknown.  
To this day, people refer to this cataclysmic event as "The Promise that Shattered the World."

**Tinkerwrights** are crafters who work a thread of mana into ordinary things. A Tinkerwright can make a small object glow, or hold a few spoken words that play back when it is tapped. Given time at the bench, they set lasting charms into gear: a keener edge, a pack that holds more than it should, a prosthetic limb that moves like flesh. Some build a small servant creature around a gemstone heart. **Field Tinkerwrights** such as Dr. Derby turn the craft to medicine. Their healer's satchels are their focus, stretched to treat far more patients than a common kit, and restocked cheaply from loose supplies. They brew doses that close wounds, steady the nerves or harden the body against harm for a day, and they move fast under fire, dragging a wounded comrade at full speed and reaching the fallen before anyone else.

### The Elves, Expanded

The elves come from a primitive homeworld. Their thousand-year lives let them witness the events that shaped their history, among them the creation of humans, a race whose uncanny likeness (save the ears and height) left the elves both intrigued and unsettled. Elven literature is of unparalleled quality: scholars of every race treat elven texts as invaluable, meticulously researched and accurate.

#### Elven Society

Elves don't build in the middle of nowhere. They find a **mana source** and live close to it, often in a cave. The less unnatural construction the better, though elven building isn't ruled out. They then build whatever is needed to maintain the mana ecosystem: protecting the animals and trees, and stopping any threat that would poison or kill the source.

Elves prefer harmony with their environment but understand necessity. They have the infrastructure of any society: waste management, agriculture and so on. Population is kept low and constant so as not to strain the source, with failsafes such as **child licenses**, and exporting elves to other settlements when there are too many or too few.

With other sentient races threatening their way of life, the elves founded the **Capital**, their most advanced society, built with the technology of a bygone era. The Capital's mana source is artificial, and looks less natural than any other elven settlement's.

#### Elven Duty: "Protect the Manasource"

Every elf learns their community's mana source and is taught to protect it from outsiders. A threat is first reported to the council, and a team is dispatched to watch it. If the outsiders plan harm, they are sabotaged: the watch team stays hidden the entire time, steals tools in the night and sets traps that look like bad luck. The moment an elf dies, the threat is to be destroyed outright, with the whole commune.

#### The Elven Life

1. **Birth.** Elves give birth like any other mammal, but need a touch of natural mana.
2. **Community care.** The community raises the young while the parents serve the commune.
3. **The first spell.** Elven children mature slowly. Humans walk and talk within 3 years; elven children take something like 30. Tied to magic, elven children often manipulate mana before they can talk.
4. **Giving to the community.** From 30 to 130, children who want to help can do clean-up or supervised gathering. There are stories of parents woken from meditation because their child was in the mood to pick up trash at midnight.
5. **The scribe years.** From 60, children are taught the world and magic, and may go on road trips to other elven settlements. This lasts until about 170.
6. **Fend for yourself.** Elves learn combat when they turn rowdy, usually between 60 and 120. The final test is to hunt an animal. If it is killed, every part must be used. If it is captured, it must be tamed; if every taming technique fails, it is released. At 170, they are formally taught advanced combat, the threats of the world, other races' fighting styles and offensive magic. Training is strict, and when it is time to fight, the community fights as one.
7. **The Pilgrimage.** At 200, an elf should leave and explore the world. Pilgrimage groups of 4 to 6 close friends travel, work and sightsee in other elven lands, binding the settlements together. Few young elves forego it.
8. **Finding a mate.** Elves are encouraged to mate with another elf. Those who mate with another race are likely excommunicated, judged case by case.
9. **The golden centuries (200–900).** By 180, an elf looks as they will for the rest of their life: perfectly symmetric, clear-skinned, thick-haired. Only their mana changes, and other elves can sense it, which they call the **Elven Sense**.
10. **Settling.** Around 500, elves settle in their home community and start families. In the Settling ritual, other elves build their home for them. They then volunteer with the Commune Council wherever they fit best.
11. **The memoir.** Between 500 and 800, elves are encouraged to write down their lives and whatever useful knowledge they found, and submit it to the Capital. If the Capital needs more, it sends a recorder.
12. **The Passing.** Elves disappear slowly. Most have written their wills long before.

Elven mating is an intricate ritual that uses a great deal of magic.

#### The Capital and its Secrets

The Capital is believed to be the landing site of the Travelers. Beneath it is a maze of tubes and objects that houses its mana source, the **Fairy Starship**. At its heart, the **Grand Academy** holds the rust-covered metallic structure, guarded by golems and opened only by the royal circlet.

The Capital is accessible only to full-blooded elves. Something of spirit or blood bars every other race from entering, half-elves included, and no one is sure what becomes of non-elves who pass the barrier. The **Capital Spies** keep its location secret through disinformation. Any elf who reveals even the border of the Capital is destroyed without a trace, and their fate is covered with misinformation. In class, elves are taught to speak of the Capital only in layers of security (magical tools that replace the speakers' words, or a guarded dialect), only with those they trust, and never before other races, orcs above all.

**Government.** A theocratic monarchy. The King, Queen, princes, princesses and a few knights form a tight royal family. The King holds immense power through the golems but is mostly ceremonial; local councils, spiritual leaders, mana guardians and elders run the smaller settlements. Leadership is often hereditary but can be earned through magical prowess, wisdom or service to elven culture. Within settlements, society is fairly egalitarian, and elders and mana specialists are most respected.

**Economy.** Small, self-sufficient populations focus on agriculture and upkeep. Elven literature, textiles and magical artifacts are prized abroad, though the elves rarely trade at scale. Select outsiders may be allowed into their libraries.

**Military.** Archers, spellcasters, spearmen, soldiers and spellswords; nearly every elf is trained in combat magic and hand-to-hand fighting. The **Capital Army** is not an invasion force but a militia with an army's strength, there to protect the library and the mana source. The King's golems guard the Capital itself.

**Arts.** Elven texts are the highest form of literature, written for both future elves and other races' scholars. Elves cherish slow, methodical music and ritual dance for the seasons and for the Passing. They also study other cultures' dances, on the principle that to know your enemy is to be able to crush them.

#### Elven Magic and Subcultures

Elves absorb their surroundings, and their magic takes on the texture of the biome that raised them. Desert elves' magic is grainy and sandy; forest elves', leaves and twigs. Dark elves' magic can't be seen, only felt, like hairs rising on your neck. Marine elves' magic smells of salt and humid warmth. In a sense, an elf is a quarter spirit and three quarters animal. Each subculture speaks a dialect close to its own language.

- **Wood Elves.** The main line during the ancient purges. Masters of detection, stealth, slings, arrows and spears, from a time when giant beasts roamed beneath the trees.
- **Dark Elves.** Cave dwellers with poor eyesight and a keen sense of touch and vibration. Their magic is exploratory, feeling out the dark with tendrils. Expert spelunkers and hunters of cave insects; they dress in stretchy climbing gear and know how to rappel.
- **Capital Elves.** Used to cobblestones, trade and settled life, they are weaker and slower than natural elves but know more spells and more weapons. **City Elves** are similar, but scorned for living among other races.
- **Tundra Elves.** Solitary, in frigid pockets, living in caves with igloos outside, eating fish caught through the ice.
- **Desert Elves.** Draw strength from sand and heat, eating plants, insects, small mammals and carrion.
- **Marine Elves.** Live with the tides in coastal or underwater settlements, eating spear-hunted fish and sea plants.

#### The Passing

An elf's passing begins when they turn intangible: hands and objects slip through them. This lasts up to a week. The elf may sit, meditate, walk or speak calmly while friends and family gather to say goodbye; though they may not react, they still hear their name. Afterwards a **bonfire** is lit and their belongings are burned in it. The metal and ash are collected, melted down and passed to the next of kin, so the elf returns to the world through the things they loved.

### Infernlings, Expanded

Infernlings are humanoid, as tall as elves or as short as halflings. They look human but for their horns, bright skin (red, blue, purple) and eyes of bright sclera with no visible pupils (red, blue, purple or yellow). Their eyes glow in the dark when caught by light, like an animal's but stronger. Common traits: horns and tail (rarely one without the other), fire resistance and the demonic language.

**Leften Infernlings** believe in a pantheon. They embrace change in all forms, work to improve the whole community and welcome outsiders.
**Denrite Infernlings** believe in a single god. They keep to their own, distrust change and live simply: each family is a community that must defend itself.

**Dovuo-Haroukt, "Dovetail"** is an Infernling monk of the **House of Basalt**. He was born with a twin whose horns interlocked with his like a dovetail joint; the twin died soon after birth. Raised an occultist in blind devotion to the House's god, he became protector of its women. When the House was raided, he woke after the attack and has followed the raiders' trail ever since, searching for his missing faith sisters. Middle-aged, skinny, greyish-purple, with shoulder-blade hair, a five o'clock shadow, large horns and a long tail. His robe is strung with basalt beads, each sheathing one of his ten darts, and he carries a shortsword. He believes the Basalt Way is the only way, is soft toward women and afraid of stern or muscular ones, and distrusts men who don't protect their "treasures". He violently punishes anyone who mocks his race. He is cruel and clever, yet tends injured birds and cats, cooks for others, sweeps the monastery steps every morning and leaves gifts on his sisters' beds. He finishes his chores by noon and meditates eight hours a day.

### Emberkin

Emberkin are fire people who constantly radiate heat. Most have orange hair and ashen skin and otherwise appear human. They can control heat in general, which makes them akin to sorcerers. They all came from a portal which has since disappeared, and so they are a rare sight: most people know them only from stories.

> ⚖ **Sources disagree.** *Jenna* and the Emberkin she grew up with say their portal is gone. The *Royal Chronicle* says Amethyst Peak’s Royal Fire Brigade still guards the Emberkin Portal. Either the Brigade guards a dead rift for prestige, or Jenna’s family never saw it.

**Pyroveil** is the fabric Emberkin use to reflect the heat their bodies radiate. It can be layered to multiply its effect, and may come from the Emberkin Plane they came from.

*A Story of a Wish.* An Infernling sage saw an enemy's Wish once. It wasn't the blinding light her master described. It was a faceless wraith ringed by warm floating candles, who summoned a ghostly blue fabric and began to cut it with scissors. With every cut the world blurred. The sage felt her strong, scarred hands shrink and soften, her height drop, her horns vanish and her ears snap short. When she went home she knew the way and kissed her husband, but he looked different: his soul felt the same, and everything else did not. She was the only magic user in her platoon, a secret she kept. She still wonders if it was a dream, or if she is dreaming still.

**Schools** taught at dedicated facilities: Abjuration (warding), Conjuration (making), Divination (knowing), Enchantment (convincing), Evocation (energy), Illusion (tricking), Transmutation (changing), **Biomancy** (life magic) and Necromancy (the forbidden magic).

**Components:** Somatic, Verbal, Material and **Thought** (mental focus).

#### What Magic Looks Like

A spell can register on any sense: sight, light, motion, hearing, smell, touch, texture, temperature, balance, pain, taste, or the feeling that something far away is close. Elves describe it best:

| Aspect | Looks like | Elven description |
| :-: | :-- | :-- |
| Raw magic | Glowing white-blue with flecks of colour; slightly cold | "Unfinished. A bard's catchy tune with no rhythm. It changes with every casting." |
| Divine (from a god) | White and yellow; warm, with a hum mistaken for choirsong | "A blanket and hot cocoa. As filling as mother's soup when you're sick." |
| Infernal (from a demon) | Red and orange; burns hot | "Holding a hot pan, getting angry it's hot, and striking with the anger." |
| Necromantic | Purple and black | "Breathing dense fog and tasting bugs. Your hand in your dead dog's throat. A sense of dread." |
| Nature or fey | Green, brown, blue or clear | "Beating the dust from a duvet and drying it in the sun. Clean, and worth it. The problem is the effort." |

Appearance depends on the **source** of the mana (its hue), the **mentor** (a style passed down: one human sorcerer's spells were red because he copied his Infernling master) and the **environment**. Elves can also *taste* magic, which can be unpleasant or dangerous, like rotten flesh.

*An elven opinion:* "My people are still studying magic, and have experiments running for a hundred years under an arcanist who cannot be replaced. The humans have done something with it, but not at our scale. How do you explain three dimensions to someone who lives in two?"

Unfinished ideas recorded with these notes: a **Great Reset** of magic, **spell customisation**, and **mana senses**.

#### The Aether System

Artheria is a closed mana ecosystem. 100 points of aether circulate through the world; nothing is gained or lost, only transmuted. Its base units are the **118 materials** (the elements), each imbued with mana. **Entities** are born from materials and mana, in five levels:

| Level | Entity | Can | Cannot |
| :-: | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 5 | God or goddess | Reshape any number of aether points, by their will or their believers' | Take a body: they appear only as an image. Forgotten gods must live in a structure or portal, or pass their power on. Inhabiting a host hastens its decay. |
| 4 | Large deity | Inspire whole races | Manifest without rituals or conditions |
| 3 | Deity | Work miracles, shape societies | Survive without worship; they go dormant |
| 2 | Patron | Protect and empower a group or place | Reach beyond their domain or followers |
| 1 | Demi-god or hero | Minor control of the aether | Escape mortal vulnerability |

Races are born from entities and inherit their traits. **Structures** built by races and entities are focal points of power and worship. **Portals** are gateways made by entities who want their own dimension; they are temporary, and carry only so much before they dissipate. **Artifacts** are defined by their aether level, from tools to legendary weapons.

### Kingdom Lore: The Red Kingdoms and the Azul Empire

**Before the Red Kingdoms**, many kingdoms, each with its own king, warred for land and resources.

**The Azul Empire.** A cut-throat businesswoman discovered a resource and monopolised it at once, using her contacts to amass an enormous fortune and with it an army: the **Azulan Army**. She merged two towns into one city in half a year, work that should have taken decades. Then she besieged and conquered one kingdom after another, once taking a walled castle in 48 hours.

**The Candlewater Conflict.** Faced with new, unknown technology, the kings set aside their differences and banded together as the **Red Kingdoms**. The war lasted a few years. Her empire was growing faster than it could hold, and a ceasefire was called. She abandoned some conquered kingdoms to fend for themselves and stayed to build up her first two towns, now one city called **Topaz**; its two halves are the **Crown** and the **Culet**. The ceasefire holds today, and many believe it is only a matter of time before the Azulan Army returns.

**The Empress's secrecy.** Her name became a secret during her rise. Rumour says she had every noble family that knew it hanged; other rumours say power drove her mad and she changes her name daily. Everyone simply calls her **the Empress**.

**The Cavalry.** Every Azulan war machine is accompanied or operated by spellcasters who hide it from sight. No one knows what the machines look like, only that they are machines. Low-ranking soldiers knew them only as "the Cavalry". Wherever the Cavalry is heard, there are rainstorms and fog, darkness by day, and Azulan soldiers struck blind, deaf, or both. Field medics were told to avoid it in every scenario.

### The Caerne's March

*A medieval fantasy mecha adventure, told by the band's own members.*

**The Caerne** is a mercenary band headed by **Yarl**. It is very old: Herald Anna says it existed long before Yarl led it, and some people have heard of the band, or of Yarl. The band votes on every initiate. Under Yarl it takes odd work: vanguard jobs for scholars over better-paying contracts, solo day jobs for coppers, ten hours a day of digging up old junk for "braniacs" at 30 gold when 200 was on offer. That made enemies inside the band.

#### The Band

- **Yarl**, "Yarl of the Coven." A human mercenary captain and spell-knight who fights with longsword, shield and crossbow and can summon his bonded weapon to his hand. He grew up in a war-torn region, rose through the camps on tactics and presence, and is loyal not to king or god but to his own code and those who serve under him. He is bound to **the Hag Sisters**, three monstrous witches who live apart and gave him a festering wound in the shape of three staves. He is close to Kaelrith.
- **The Herald Anna.** An elf woman, the band's bard and spokesperson, who finds missions through noble contacts. Platinum-blonde, pale and waifish, she speaks as little as possible, and is prejudiced against orcs, as is common among elves. A few seconds before she speaks, those looking at her hear the world go quiet and her voice swell (**auditory masking**). She uses illusion to change her looks, writes glowing lines in the air to present information, and is skilled with a disguise kit.
- **Kaelrith, "Kayla."** An Owlkin caster with white feathers, potentially a teenager. She formally wears white robes with the Church of the First's symbol. Quiet, awkward and blunt at first; she loves books and music and once left an outing to hear her favourite bard. She was seen carrying a **human finger preserved in yellow oil**, and when asked, no one in camp knows anything about it. She talks to Yarl like a childhood friend.
- **Hawk**, a Leovar scout who goes ahead before every deployment. "Every silver counts."
- **Onager.** A 6'8" half-orc, 235 pounds, a former **City Watch investigator** who ran afoul of corrupt bureaucrats in a big city and turned mercenary. He fights unarmed and sees in the dark. "It's not enough to know it all; you have to be accurate."
- **Iron-Tail.** A black-furred **Nezzari** (ratfolk, to outsiders), proud of her groomed fur, muscular from years planting lotus seeds in the swamp. Raised by heretical but loving iconoclast parents, she was never pious enough to rise in the Nezzari hierarchy and was content to farm and scavenge. Then a **monstrous beast with acid saliva** rampaged through her village, and she drove it off with nothing but a shovel. Knighted for it, she found the burden strangely comfortable: in a world without gods, she believes, heroes are needed most. She is a paladin of the **Oath of the Mech Rider** and a part-time engineer with a knack for machines.
- **Dr. Derby Chadwick Merriweather.** An eccentric, unstable and undeniably brilliant medic in his early forties: clean-shaven, short neat hair, small round spectacles, and a long white overcoat over studded leather and an apron. His big weathered gloves are permanently stained with dried blood, and he moves with quiet precision, never without a sturdy leather suitcase and a backpack full of tools. Nobles with rare or incurable diseases seek him as a last resort; his cures are infamous for being unconventional and often grotesque. He chose the battlefield because it asks fewer questions of his experiments, and there he tends the wounded with genuine care and twisted curiosity in equal measure. Once an **Azulan field medic**, his unit was abandoned in Red Kingdom territory, and he knows first-hand the monstrous force of his old comrades. The Azulan Army was not evil, he says: they healed who they could, and in the chaos of triage he even healed **Etan Pendragon**. He hid all his soldier's gear except his **cavalry rapier**, light, dense steel; the only tell is the hilt, carved with a gemstone, with a lanyard hole where a deep-blue tassel used to hang.
- **Jenna Flame Dancer.** An Emberkin woman, once a carnival knife thrower ("a Fire Dancer"). Her family came through a portal with other Emberkin long ago, and her people use Pyroveil to keep from burning everything around them. Shy at first, bubbly once comfortable, silly and a little dense; she wears disguises for fun and once left a scorched imprint of her rear on a tavern bench. She cannot read. With her share of 12,000 gold, she planned to return home.
- **Gilda**, a halfling engineer, joined with the newer members.

> ⚖ **Sources disagree.** *Camp gossip* guessed Kaelrith a teenager from her size. *Iron-Tail* learned she is one of the band’s oldest members and Yarl’s childhood friend. Owlkin ages are hard to read.

#### Former Members

- **Rupert**, a fat mercenary who tried to blackmail Derby for 500 gold. Derby set his tent on fire and Rupert died in it. The band, vulgar and uncaring, said he simply died cooking meat for himself in his tent.
- **Karaku**, a charmer of tavern women who talked of his wife (golden hair, unworldly grace) long after she left him. A captured, crippled enemy cavalier, whom the band had spared and nursed, escaped his binds the morning he was to be released and cut Karaku's throat. No one saw a thing.
- **Torgon**, a half-orc veteran with war scars across his shoulders. He wanted the band to stop sparing prisoners and stop taking charity jobs, and plotted with half the camp to vote Yarl out and sell him for the bounties on his head. **Torgon's Uprising** failed; his body broken, he was hanged as an example. Everyone pulled double shifts for weeks after.

Camp talk from those days also mentions **High-Pox**, a nasty disease that Derby kept out of camp by turning away a band of sick street children.

#### The Telandor Savior

On a princess-rescue contract organised by Anna, the band cleared a goblin cave and its boss, a hairless bugbear. In the next cavern, the goblins had heaped their treasure as tribute around a giant statue with a topaz at its crown. Hawk slipped, the cave began to collapse, and **Iron-Tail fell into the statue**. It lit up blue, rumbled, and closed around her: squares of light opened, and she found herself looking down at the party from inside, cool as an air-conditioned room.

It is a titan in the shape of a knight frame, 10 feet tall, big enough for Iron-Tail to stand in, and appears to be **elvish technology from thousands of years ago**. Iron-Tail named it the **Telandor Savior**: "Telandor" is Elvish for *iron*, and "Savior" means just that in Common.

The band split on it. Anna said keep it and learn it; Hawk said sell it to the Aethermancers; Kaelrith said it was too big and would slow them down; Jenna said find someone to teach them; the princess called it "a knight in shining armour for a whole town". Yarl kept it hidden in the treeline, and the princess went home first.

#### The 'Disbanding'

With the titan's discovery, Yarl set fire to the Caerne camp and disbanded the group, though its members still travel together. Unseen, they left **Carmine Reach** in the middle of a feast held in their honour, and travelled to **Boilmarsh**, Iron-Tail's home in the swamps.

#### Goblins, as Derby Knows Them

Goblins are the universal pests of the world: three feet tall, green, known in every culture to steal and pillage wherever they can. They cannot be reasoned with and care for nothing but hunger and breeding, and their inbred litters mature within a single season. One alone dies to a good kick, but they are clever with tools, traps and terrain, and some have learned the bow by watching adventurers. As an tinkerwright, Derby knows the truth: goblins are the byproducts of a **Mad Tinkerwright** from long ago, the same one responsible for the creation of orcs. Goblins are failed orcs.

> ⚖ **Sources disagree.** *Derby’s Tinkerwright teachers* credit one Mad Tinkerwright with making orcs and goblins alike. *Talar the Human* says orcs came from human Ashen Magic, and *Quimzy the Orc* says goblins are cast-out runts. Every trade blames its own legend.

### Kavik's Band

**Kavik Zamir** is remembered as the first warlock of the Patron: a strikingly handsome male Infernling with long, shaggy brown hair, a fitted robe that bared his body, leather bracers and a red sash. He followed a group into a dungeon and there met the Patron. After some rehabilitation, he set out to adventure, and became the de facto leader of a band whose every member met an untimely end.

> ⚖ **Sources disagree.** *Kavik’s admirers* call him the Patron’s first warlock. The *Scroll of Infernling Origin* gives that title to the Scholar Pendragon, ages earlier. The admirers mean the first of this age, or never read the Scroll.

- **Hans, "Hands."** A young monk woman, the hero of her town, who fought Kavik, lost, and followed him for a rematch. Later she was crippled, and no magic could reverse it. In shame she left, lived on the streets off scraps, and then disappeared.
- **Blue.** A slim, elegant royal with thick dark-brown hair tied in a messy ponytail with a blue ribbon, and dark eyes that turn opal green in the sun. When the enemy began killing royals and commoners alike, soldiers hid her among them in a baggy conscript's uniform, with a blue cap and grime on her face to pass as a boy. Trained as an arbalist, she carried a five-bolt magazine crossbow that broke down into her belt pack and had an extendable rifled barrel for sharpshooting. She went looking for her friends, the prince and princess, and became a beacon of hope as a sniper. Before the war she was an accomplished pianist and loved to cook. After the war she returned to her kingdom's court, but a coup came: she was publicly executed in a way that broke the rebellion's spirit, her family's holdings razed, and her body burned with the wood of her own piano. Some blame Kavik's Patron. Some say her kingdom was the Azul Empire.
- **Bones.** A revenant skeleton swashbuckler, a mercenary cursed by a genie-like creature. In sailor's clothes, he is a walking corpse, smell and all: red bones wet with fresh blood, a hole in his ribs showing gore, faint lights in his sockets, sea salt and rotten meat. In his cover form he is a frighteningly focused man with sunken eyes who never breathes. He needed no sleep and kept the camp's watch, bound to Kavik for rescuing his **phylactery**. Bones was instrumental in ending the war through his immortality, his bones replaced with metal or rebuilt with metal glue, and that war made Kavik a world-renowned hero.

A decade of adventure followed. Then Kavik settled down and had a son with an Infernling woman. He went to a place called Ranlean when the settlement was still only a few buildings cobbled together. In the first year of his retirement he tried to free Bones, but the creature that cursed Bones broke and quartered him and threw him, phylactery and all, into a whirlpool. For years after, Bones was a local legend that kept children from swimming too far into the current. That was the last time Kavik held his spear. News of Blue's death reached him years late; the settlement had little contact with the outside world.

> ⚖ **Sources disagree.** The *Lay of Kavik’s Band*, a Ranlean ballad, has Kavik retire in Ranlean and decline for years, blind and deaf. *Cerwind’s account* of Kazka (above) has him retire in Aralon and die soon after passing on the Patron. Ranlean’s bards like to claim heroes for their town, and Cerwind heard the story from a grieving son.

Kavik handed his patron connection to his six-year-old son, **Kazka**, believing it would shield the boy from the Patron's magnetic pull of misfortune. From that day, Kavik never heard the Patron's voice again. At 17, Kazka left to adventure with his father's spear, and only after Kazka had lost much did the Patron begin speaking to him as it once had to Kavik. Kavik lost his sight and hearing. While Kazka was away, his caretakers abused him, and he died in bed, neglected and starved, with stray predators eating most of his body. Kazka last saw his father at 24, after Jerry's death, when the letters about his father's health suddenly stopped. By the time he returned, his father had long since died, and his treasures were stolen and sold. Once he was the hero of the world. He died a miserable death.

#### Kazka and his Friends, Later

Kazka at 38 is a cranky paladin of Petrichor who soaks at the **Aralon Hot Springs** and keeps a daily memento mori. **Fumir** left his side, partly tired of that inconsistent life; she later returned as a diplomat for another Infernling group, and had become a symbol of hope because of the deaths she saw while with Kazka. Some say she has something to do with the Azul Empire.

**Candor Nockcrest** is a half-elven rogue of noble birth, familiar with the elves and their history since he is half one.

### The Shrine of the First (A Twoshot)

**Tobias Tenderfoot**, an aristocratic gnome with an Irish lilt, spent years earning gold and making allies to hire his "elite team": **Clive**, an aloof human duskstalker and sharpshooter; **Sha'kill**, a 7-foot Titankin runeguard who seeks glory and doesn't seem to know what gold is for; and **Kraven**, a parrot-like Aviari druid of Sylvaen, promised sanctuaries of trees in Sylvaen's name. Tobias carried a map in Gnomish and a foot-long golden key with six wings and a blue gem, and sought a relic in one of the lost **Shrines of the First**.

In his youth Tobias served "the Short Kings", royals short even by gnomish standards, until his party fell to the all-women **House of Basalt**. A white light from a Church of the First deacon blasted the executioner and saved him. He spent the rest of his fortune serving Ein, the one-winged god. The relic, he believed, could bring Ein back, and the House of Basalt would kill for it.

On the road, a southeastern invasion force was marching northwest. At Felidae the party chose the expensive toll roads, guarded by catfolk towers. They passed a razed town of 500 and took on a silent, soot-covered mother and her baby. Two days later, a goblin trap (a 5-foot pit dug across the road) tipped the carriage, and goblins with a giant wolf attacked. Kraven's thorns and Sylvaen's column of light, Sha'kill's slam into the trees and Clive's arrows won the fight, but the wolf had mauled Tobias. Dying, he gave Clive the map and key: "Find my love, Ms. Tenderfoot. A cottage in the grove, in the East. Tell her exactly this: 'It seems my luck's run out, angel.' Bring her what you find in the Shrine of the First." The mother and child were gone.

Two factions hunt the relic: the **Strength Brigade**, where orcs and Titankins enslave goblins and smaller folk, and the **Animal Kingdom**, almost entirely beastmen. The relic is **the Sacred Flame**, an unending fire in a small brazier, held in a shrine of ancient runes powered by hand-held mana figures.

### Manascrap: A Far Future

*One telling of what Artheria might become.* The races of Artheria banded together and reached peace, with planes, super-yachts, building-sized mechanical suits and telescopes that saw other worlds. Then someone pulled the atomic trigger. Eighty years later, animals and monsters live alongside the people in the sprawl. The city spreads 70 miles and goes a mile underground, its decaying industry overgrown with vines, giant robots standing forgotten in the mist, and people using technology they no longer understand.

Magic is still around, but no one uses it. Whatever killed the people and plants killed the magic too: it is toxic, smells, and comes out like gunk. A firebolt may also spit flecks of rust that cut the caster and leave an infection. Everyone's mana veins are clogged, so even the old magic doors no longer open. The real magic now belongs to the **Technomancers**, the **Mages of the Script**. The original code mages died at the moment of impact, and the handful left know how to open the sealed vaults of non-radioactive material, protected by magically dense metals. They hide, and they keep a code.
